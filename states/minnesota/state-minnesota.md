# Minnesota Cannabis Regulatory Profile

## Overview

Minnesota's adult-use cannabis market was established by the legislature via HF 100 (2023) — not a ballot initiative — making it uniquely susceptible to legislative gridlock and mid-course rulemaking revisions. The Office of Cannabis Management (OCM), housed under the Department of Revenue, administers adult-use; the Department of Health (DOH) continues to run the medical program under Chapter 152. Retail launched mid-2025 after a slower-than-expected rollout driven by OCM organizational challenges and protracted rulemaking. The compliance environment is shaped by three overlapping tensions: a legislature that passed adult-use reluctantly, a social equity mandate that reserves majority license shares for qualifying applicants, and an unregulated hemp-derived cannabinoid market that competes with the licensed system.

## Regulatory Structure

### Dual Agency Authority
Minnesota operates **two parallel cannabis regulatory systems**:

| Agency | Program | Statutory Authority | Key Distinction |
|--------|---------|---------------------|----------------|
| Office of Cannabis Management (OCM) | Adult-Use | Chapter 390 (HF 100) | Licensing, enforcement, rulemaking |
| Department of Health (DOH) | Medical | Chapter 152 | Patient registry, medical dispensaries |

**Compliance implication:** Operators holding both adult-use and medical endorsements maintain **separate inventory tracks** in METRC. The OCM and DOH do not share enforcement jurisdiction — a violation with medical inventory is a DOH matter, not OCM.

### OCM Structure
- Director appointed by Commissioner of Revenue
- Divisions: Licensing, Enforcement, Social Equity, Testing & Monitoring
- Advisory Council on Cannabis (stakeholder input)
- Rules under Minn. R. Ch. 8200–8215 (permanent rules still in development as of 2025)

### Legal Hierarchy
1. Minnesota Constitution
2. Minnesota Statutes Chapter 390 (adult-use) + Chapter 152 (medical)
3. OCM/DOH Administrative Rules
4. Local ordinances (municipal opt-outs)

### Social Equity Licensing
- **50%+ of licenses** reserved for social equity applicants
- Qualifying criteria: disproportionately affected areas (history of cannabis enforcement), veterans, small farmers, legacy market participants
- Reduced application and license fees
- Technical assistance programs
- Community reinvestment fund allocation from tax revenue

## Seed-to-Sale Tracking

- **System:** METRC (statewide mandate for all licensees)
- **Scope:** Plant tagging (RFID from propagation), harvest, processing, packaging, transfer manifests, sales, waste
- **Reporting windows:** 24-hour reporting for harvest events; daily inventory reconciliation; monthly OCM reporting
- **Discrepancy protocol:** 48-hour root cause investigation required; OCM notification within 24 hours of discovery
- **Audit retention:** 5-year minimum
- **System failure:** Manual logging required; enter within 24 hours of restoration
- **Medical vs. adult-use separation:** Each program maintains separate METRC inventory categories — product cannot be transferred between programs without documented repackaging event with METRC entry
- **Tribal operations:** METRC not required for tribal enterprises on tribal land

## Key Statutes

- **Minn. Stat. Chapter 390** — Primary adult-use framework (HF 100, 2023)
- **Minn. Stat. §152.22–152.37** — Medical cannabis program
- **Minn. Stat. §181.938** — Employment protections for lawful off-duty conduct (including cannabis)
- **Minn. Stat. §181.938** — Hemp-derived cannabinoid employment context
- **Minn. R. Ch. 8200–8215** — OCM administrative rules (permanent rules in progress)
- **HF 100 (2023 Session)** — Enabling legislation for Chapter 390

## Tax Architecture

| Tax | Rate | Applied To |
|-----|------|-----------|
| Gross Receipts Tax | 10% | Retail sale price |
| Standard Sales Tax | ~6.875–8.875% | Pre-gross-receipts price |
| **Combined Consumer Rate** | ~16–19% | Varies by jurisdiction |

**Operator margin note:** The 10% gross receipts tax is applied to the full retail price — operators cannot deduct it from the gross receipts base for sales tax purposes. Modeling margin around the gross receipts figure alone materially understates tax burden.
