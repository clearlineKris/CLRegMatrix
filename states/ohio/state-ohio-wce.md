# Ohio WCE Field Operations Protocol

## Overview

Working Class Exhaustive (WCE) field operations for Ohio cannabis compliance. This document translates statutory requirements and DCC operational guidance into step-by-step operational protocols for field personnel. The central compliance task for Ohio dual-use operators is METRC workflow reconfiguration — not a technical upgrade but the core compliance task of the dual-use transition.

## Seed-to-Sale Protocols (METRC)

**System:** METRC (carried over from medical program under ORC Chapter 3796; now operates under dual-use framework)

**Critical:** Adult-use and medical inventory tracked in **separate METRC categories**. Transfer manifests must specify receiving license type. Product conversion between designations requires documented repackaging event.

---

### Core Workflows

#### 1. Plant Tagging
- **Trigger:** New plants received or germinated
- **Action:** Assign METRC RFID tag to each plant; designate category (medical or adult-use)
- **Proof:** METRC tag assignment record, plant count log
- **Deadline:** Within 24 hours of receipt/germination
- **Authority:** ORC §3780, DCC Rules

#### 2. Growth Phase Transition
- **Trigger:** Plants enter flowering (vegetative → flowering)
- **Action:** Update plant status in METRC; verify category designation
- **Proof:** METRC growth phase log
- **Deadline:** Same day as transition
- **Authority:** DCC Rules 4700-series

#### 3. Harvest Event
- **Trigger:** Plants harvested
- **Action:** Record wet weight in METRC; create harvest batch; route to drying/curing
- **Proof:** METRC harvest record, chain of custody
- **Deadline:** Same day as harvest
- **Authority:** ORC §3780

#### 4. Package Creation & Labeling
- **Trigger:** Product packaged for transfer or sale
- **Action:** Create package in METRC; apply DCC-compliant label; assign to correct category (medical/adult-use)
- **Proof:** METRC package record, label photo
- **Deadline:** Before any transfer or sale
- **Authority:** DCC packaging rules

#### 5. Transfer Manifest (Dual-Use Complexity)
- **Trigger:** Product leaves facility
- **Action:** Generate METRC manifest specifying **receiving license type** (medical vs. adult-use); separate manifests for each license type
- **Proof:** METRC manifest, transport log
- **Deadline:** Before transport
- **Authority:** ORC §3780, DCC transport rules
- **Critical:** Medical-to-adult-use or adult-use-to-medical conversion requires documented repackaging event in METRC before any transfer

#### 6. Sales Transaction (Dual-Use)
- **Trigger:** Customer purchase
- **Action:** Log transaction in METRC under correct inventory category; verify customer age 21+ (adult-use) or valid medical card (medical)
- **Proof:** METRC sales record, POS receipt
- **Deadline:** Real-time
- **Authority:** ORC §3780

#### 7. Waste Creation & Destruction
- **Trigger:** Waste event (trim, fail, expired)
- **Action:** Log waste in METRC; document destruction method
- **Proof:** METRC waste record, photo documentation
- **Deadline:** Same day as waste creation
- **Authority:** DCC waste rules

## Dual-Use Conversion Protocol

For operators converting from medical-only to dual-use:

1. **Pre-conversion audit:** Review METRC inventory; categorize existing stock as medical-only
2. **Category separation plan:** Document how medical and adult-use inventory will be physically and digitally separated
3. **METRC workflow update:** Configure distinct inventory categories; update transfer manifest templates; train staff on separate tracking
4. **Camera coverage review:** DCC requires updated camera angles for dual-use facilities; common failure point in early inspections
5. **Signage update:** Adult-use facilities require specific signage; medical and adult-use areas must be clearly delineated
6. **Conversion application:** Submit amended application to DCC with compliance plan
7. **DCC inspection:** Schedule and pass compliance inspection; correct any findings before commencing adult-use sales
8. **Soft launch:** Begin adult-use sales with separate METRC tracking workflows confirmed operational

## Transport / Delivery Protocols

### Pre-Trip
1. Verify METRC manifest matches cargo
2. Confirm receiving license number and type
3. Verify vehicle GPS active
4. Confirm camera system operational
5. Check lock/seal integrity

### In-Transit
1. Maintain GPS tracking at all times
2. No unscheduled stops
3. Cargo stays locked
4. Driver maintains manifest copy

### Delivery / Receipt
1. Recipient verifies METRC manifest against physical cargo
2. Recipient confirms license-type designation on manifest matches their license
3. Any discrepancy → do not complete transfer; contact DCC and home office
4. Both parties sign manifest copy; retain for records

### Contingencies
| Scenario | Response |
|----------|---------|
| Vehicle breakdown | Notify DCC immediately; document; arrange replacement transport |
| Cargo seal broken | Report to DCC; document all contents; investigate before proceeding |
| Delivery rejection | Return product to origin; log in METRC; document reason |
| Accident | Notify DCC within 24 hours; preserve cargo; cooperate with investigation |

## Enforcement Response Playbooks

### Scenario A: Routine DCC Inspection

1. **Receive notice** — DCC typically provides advance notice for routine inspections; confirm appointment in writing
2. **Prepare documentation** — METRC records, transfer manifests, COAs, waste records, visitor logs, camera footage
3. **Pre-inspection walkthrough** — Walk your facility against the inspection checklist before the inspector arrives
4. **During inspection** — Cooperate fully; provide requested documents within reasonable time; do not volunteer information beyond what's asked
5. **Common findings in early Ohio dual-use inspections:**
   - METRC category separation errors
   - Camera angle gaps (especially post-conversion)
   - Outdated signage (medical-only signage not updated for dual-use)
   - Transfer manifest license-type designation errors
6. **After inspection** — If findings issued, correct immediately and document correction; request written confirmation of cure
7. **Repeat findings** — DCC escalation matrix applies; do not assume education-first posture continues after first round

### Scenario B: Complaint-Driven Investigation

1. **Receive subpoena/interview request** — DCC notifying of complaint; do not ignore
2. **Do not self-incriminate** — Provide requested documents but consult compliance counsel before giving oral statements
3. **Preserve all records** — Litigation hold on METRC data, POS records, communications
4. **Internal investigation** — Identify scope of potential violation; correct if ongoing
5. **DCC response** — Cooperate but document everything; request written findings
6. **Escalation risk** — If inventory diversion or sales to minors alleged, assume zero-tolerance applies

### Scenario C: Administrative Action / License Proceeding

1. **Notice of violation** — DCC issues formal notice; response deadline is typically 30 days
2. **Request hearing** — All license actions are entitled to administrative hearing; exercise this right
3. **Prepare defense** — Document compliance history, corrective actions taken, systemic vs. isolated issue
4. **Penalties** — Range from warning → fine → license suspension → license revocation depending on severity and history
5. **Dual-use consideration** — Actions on one license type (medical or adult-use) may affect the other; address both
