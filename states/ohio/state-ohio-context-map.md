# Ohio Context Map

## Overview

Ohio's adult-use cannabis market was created by voter approval in November 2023 and is being actively reconstructed by the legislature that opposed it. The Division of Cannabis Control stands up a dual-use program under conditions of genuine inter-branch legal conflict. Compliance strategy in Ohio cannot be separated from political intelligence — the ground shifts monthly.

## Common Operator Mistakes in Ohio

1. **Porting medical POS logic to adult-use** — Adult-use point-of-sale is not governed by the cumulative allotment structure that medical operators built their compliance systems around. Operators who applied medical tracking logic to adult-use retail receive DCC corrective notices.

2. **Assuming unified METRC tracking is sufficient** — Adult-use and medical inventory must be tracked in separate METRC categories. Transfer manifests must specify receiving license type. Even product stored in the same facility must be in distinct categories. Operators who assumed a unified tracking workflow fail inspection.

3. **Treating home grow provisions as settled law** — Issue 2 established 6 plants/person, 12/household. Legislative amendments added registration requirements. The compliance trap around harvest possession limits (excess harvest = violation) and the unresolved enforcement mechanism create legal risk that operators are ignoring.

4. **Signing leases in opted-out jurisdictions** — Dozens of municipalities opted out with no advance notice. Operators who signed leases before verifying opt-out status found commercial viability eliminated overnight.

5. **Underestimating combined tax burden** — Modeling margin on the 10% excise rate alone results in material mispricing. The ~17% combined consumer rate changes competitive dynamics.

## Strategic Notes for Veridion Operators

1. **Ohio is a political intelligence market, not a compliance certainty market.** Monitor legislative session calendar. Track every committee vote on cannabis-related bills. Build relationships with DCC staff.

2. **METRC workflow reconfiguration is the central compliance task.** Budget for it as a core operational expense, not an IT upgrade. Staff for it. Test it before you need it.

3. **Dual-use complexity will increase inspection scrutiny.** The DCC's early inspection teams are specifically looking for category separation errors. If you're running both programs, your METRC data is being examined more closely than a single-license operation.

4. **Municipal opt-out risk is a lease risk.** Verify before executing. Build delivery capability as a hedge.

5. **Hemp-derived grey market is structural competition.** Until DCC rules are finalized, licensed operators face price competition from unregulated Delta-8 and THCA products. This affects pricing strategy and demand forecasting.

6. **Equity operators face structural headwinds.** The equity tools (fee reductions, priority processing) reduce friction but don't close the capital and infrastructure gap. Support strategies should acknowledge this.
