# Ohio Penumbrant Paper

## Overview

Ohio's grey areas are not primarily generated by ambiguous statutory language — they are generated by **active political conflict between what voters approved and what the legislature wants to impose**. This document maps the regulatory gray zones where operators must exercise judgment because the law is silent, contradictory, or actively contested.

---

## Seven Penumbral Zones — Ohio Assessment

---

### Zone 1: Federal-State Conflict Vector
**Risk Score: 25/25 (Critical)**

| Factor | Status | Risk |
|--------|--------|------|
| CSA Schedule I | Active | Banking, 280E, gun rights |
| SAFE Act | Not passed | No federal banking protection |
| 280E Tax Burden | Active | Effective tax rate 40-80% |
| Federal Employment | Prohibited | Clearance/contracting impacts |

**Ohio-Specific Issues:**
- Federal property (Wright-Patterson AFB, federal buildings in Columbus, Cincinnati, Cleveland)
- DOT-regulated positions (commercial driver's licenses, transit authorities)
- Federal contractors in aerospace and defense supply chains

**Decision Tree: Federal Conflict Encounter**
```
Federal nexus identified?
├─ YES → Employment-related?
│   ├─ YES → Federal clearance/DOT required?
│   │   ├─ YES → Cannabis prohibited; no state protection
│   │   └─ NO → Document lawful off-duty use
│   └─ NO → Banking/tax?
│       ├─ YES → 280E applies; credit union banking
│       └─ NO → Gun rights? (ATF Form 4473 prohibits users)
└─ NO → Standard state compliance applies
```

### Zone 2: Hemp-Derived Intoxicant Gray Zone
**Risk Score: 18/25 (High)**

| Factor | Status | Risk |
|--------|--------|------|
| Delta-8 THC | Unregulated | Sold in tobacco shops, convenience stores |
| THCA | Unregulated | Sold as "hemp" flower |
| HHC, THCP | Unregulated | Novel compounds, no testing |
| Age Verification | Not required (hemp) | Youth access concern |
| Testing Standards | None established | Consumer safety gap |

**Ohio-Specific Issues:**
- Hemp-derived products sold alongside tobacco and in vape shops statewide
- No licensing pathway for hemp-derived intoxicant retailers
- DCC has indicated intent to regulate but formal rulemaking pending
- Licensed cannabis operators face unfair competition from unregulated hemp market
- No clear boundary between legal hemp and illegal intoxicating cannabinoids

**Decision Tree: Hemp-Derived Product Encounter**
```
Product with intoxicating cannabinoid encountered?
├─ YES → Sold as "hemp" (<0.3% Delta-9)?
│   ├─ YES → Does total THC exceed threshold?
│   │   ├─ YES → Likely illegal under ORC 3780; report to DCC
│   │   └─ NO → Legal hemp; no cannabis license required
│   └─ NO → In licensed cannabis facility?
│       ├─ YES → Must be in METRC with test batch
│       └─ NO → Unlicensed sale; enforcement risk
└─ NO → Standard hemp; no action needed
```

### Zone 3: Local Control & Zoning Penumbra
**Risk Score: 20/25 (High)**

| Factor | Status | Risk |
|--------|--------|------|
| Municipal Opt-Out | Allowed | Dozens of jurisdictions opted out or moratorium |
| Buffer Zones | State minimum | Local can increase beyond state minimum |
| Cultivation Ban | Preempted | State does not allow local ban |
| Delivery Ban | Preempted | State does not allow local ban |
| Commercial Map Stability | Low | Council can change with single session vote |

**Ohio-Specific Issues:**
- Municipal opt-outs fragmenting commercial geography in suburban corridors — the most commercially attractive retail footprints
- Opt-out decisions made by ballot initiative OR local legislative action with no advance notice and no rulemaking process
- Urban cores (Columbus, Cleveland, Cincinnati) and permissive rural jurisdictions receiving displaced operators
- Commercial map is **genuinely unstable** — a jurisdiction viable today can change with a single council session

**Decision Tree: Municipal Opt-Out Encounter**
```
Planning to operate in municipality?
├─ YES → Check opt-out status immediately before signing leases
│   ├─ Opted out → Can delivery reach that jurisdiction?
│   │   ├─ YES → Delivery-only model may bypass opt-out
│   │   └─ NO → Do not pursue; commercial viability = zero
│   └─ NO → Verify buffer zones (school/church proximity)
└─ NO → Unincorporated area? State minimums apply
```

### Zone 4: Home Cultivation Trap
**Risk Score: 16/20 (High)**

Ohio's home cultivation provisions contain a compliance trap the DCC has not resolved through formal guidance.

| Issue | Detail |
|-------|--------|
| Statutory baseline | Issue 2: 6 plants/person, 12/household |
| Legislative modification | Registration requirements, security mandates, proximity restrictions |
| Enforcement mechanism | **Genuinely unclear** — local law enforcement has limited appetite for residential inspections |
| Privacy exposure | Registry creates civil liberties concern; advocates argue it causes more harm than the activity it's meant to govern |
| Harvest compliance trap | Harvested cannabis counts toward personal possession limit (2.5 oz). Growers who harvest more than that at one time are technically in violation unless excess was destroyed or is still attached to living plants. |

**Operational implication for licensed operators:** Home grow provisions shape demand modeling for flower products. Ambiguity around enforcement creates a grey-market supply channel that licensed retail cannot control or predict.

### Zone 5: Medical vs. Adult-Use Boundary
**Risk Score: 15/20 (Moderate-High)**

| Factor | Status | Risk |
|--------|--------|------|
| Separate Programs | ORC 3796 vs ORC 3780 | Dual regulatory framework |
| Medical Operators | ~130 dispensaries, 40+ cultivators | First in line for dual-use conversion |
| Conversion Process | Streamlined on paper | Early inspection findings: camera angles, signage, METRC reconfiguration |
| Inventory Separation | Required | Separate METRC categories; distinct transfer manifests; repackaging for designation conversion |
| Early Inspection Focus | Medical-adult-use separation | Central compliance task of dual-use transition |

**The Incumbency Problem:** Medical operators who converted first are overwhelmingly white-owned businesses that entered under HB 523 — a program with no equity provisions. These incumbents hold structural first-mover advantage in facility approvals, supply chains, and years of regulatory familiarity.

---

### Zone 6: Enforcement Climate Uncertainty
**Risk Score: 14/20 (Moderate)**

| Factor | Status | Risk |
|--------|--------|------|
| Early enforcement stance | Education-first | Reduced near-term risk for good-faith operators |
| Zero-tolerance carve-outs | Active from day one | Sales to minors, inventory diversion, METRC failures treated identically for all operators |
| DCC inspection focus | METRC category separation | Operators who assumed unified tracking fail review |
| Legislative threat | Active | Senate Bill 261 amendments can introduce new violations retroactively |

**The Three-Layer Problem:** Operators must track Issue 2 statute, legislative amendments, and DCC interpretations as three separate, sometimes contradictory layers — and any of those layers can shift with the next committee vote.

---

### Zone 7: Equity Program Structural Limitations
**Risk Score: 12/20 (Moderate)**

| Factor | Status | Risk |
|--------|--------|------|
| Equity provisions | Issue 2 included | Modified by legislature |
| Program application | New licenses only | Does not retroactively restructure medical licensees |
| Equity tools | Fee reductions, priority processing, technical assistance | Real advantages at the margins |
| Fundamental gap | Capital and infrastructure | Application-stage head starts don't close the gap |
| Two-tier market | Structural | Existing market controlled by incumbents from non-equity program |

## Key Recommendations

1. **Federal Risk:** Prioritize credit union banking; document 280E compliance; identify any federal employee or contractor nexus in workforce
2. **Hemp-Derived:** Monitor DCC rulemaking; do not carry unregulated products in licensed facilities; report violations to DCC
3. **Local Zoning:** Verify opt-out status before executing leases; maintain delivery capability as hedge against opt-out jurisdiction
4. **Home Grow:** Do not advise clients to treat home grow as settled law; flag the compliance trap around harvest possession limits
5. **Medical/Adult-Use:** If operating both, maintain strict METRC category separation; treat conversion requirements as hard compliance, not paperwork
6. **Enforcement:** Assume METRC category separation is being audited on every inspection; configure workflows for dual-use complexity from day one
7. **Equity:** Acknowledge the structural limitation; focus on what the equity tools can actually accomplish at the margins
